# Field profile

The reader develops machine-learning interatomic potentials and uses molecular dynamics to study molten salts, solid–liquid electrochemical interfaces, and doped MoS2. The work connects atomic structure and free energies to transport, thermodynamics, environmental reactivity, friction, and wear.

## Core (always save)

- Machine-learning interatomic potentials: MACE, DeepMD-kit, UMA, foundation-model fine-tuning, multi-head replay, LoRA, equivariant models, and accuracy or transferability benchmarks.
- Active learning and training data: uncertainty-guided sampling, bias forces, iterative DFT labeling, automated frame selection, and training sets for complex interfaces and liquids.
- Molten salt structure and thermodynamics: LiCl–FeCl2, LiCl–KCl, coordination polyhedra, Fe–Fe clustering, transport properties, Kirkwood–Buff theory, phase diagrams, and high-energy X-ray diffraction.
- Electrochemical interfaces and electrolytes: electrode–electrolyte and solid–liquid interfaces, ionic solutions, electrode–ion interactions, ion transport in solid and quasi-solid electrolytes (for example Li⁺ hopping in MOFs), free-energy profiles, and enhanced-sampling molecular dynamics.
- MoS2, 2D materials, and tribology: doped and defective MoS2, TMD electronics, environmental reactivity, dry-film lubricants, friction and wear mechanisms, low-temperature behavior, and sputter-deposited films.
- Atomistic simulation and validation: periodic DFT, VASP, ab initio molecular dynamics, LAMMPS, ASE, and comparisons of simulated structure or transport with experiments.
- Atomistic foundation models and datasets: FAIR Chemistry, OMat, OMol, OC20/OC25, MatterGen, PET-MAD and MAD, universal potentials, catalysis datasets, reduced-equivariance architectures, benchmarks, and generalization across chemical compositions.
- Generative and AI-for-chemistry/materials methods: Boltzmann generators, diffusion or flow models for molecules and crystals, inverse design, and machine-learning materials databases (for example perovskite datasets).

## Adjacent (save, lower priority)

- HPC and exascale computing for science: ALCF, Aurora, Polaris, SC conferences, GPU scaling, new leadership-class systems and AI testbeds, partnerships that open these systems to researchers, and AI accelerators for scientific computing (for example Cerebras wafer-scale systems).
- AI agents for science, self-driving labs, and scientific studies of AI agents themselves (for example a preprint measuring how coding agents spend tokens or fail).
- Scientific machine learning: neural operators, reduced-order models, physics-informed ML, and ML-guided screening of simulation designs.
- Scientific computing workflows: high-throughput DFT and molecular dynamics, GPU scaling, MPI, Slurm, and research applications on Aurora or Polaris. Save substantive technical talks on scientific AI infrastructure, such as scaling PyTorch on Intel XPU across Aurora.
- Materials characterization: tribometry and X-ray scattering studies that explain friction, wear, liquid structure, or coordination in relevant materials.
- Related atomistic materials mechanics: simulations and machine-learning analyses of strength, deformation, defects, grain structure, and nanoscale mechanics in alloys. Examples include rapid solidification of Inconel 718, solder materials, and the effects of grain size, grain morphology, and nanovoids on the strength of nanocrystalline 17-4 PH steel.
- Research venues: Journal of Chemical Theory and Computation, The Journal of Chemical Physics, Journal of Materials Chemistry A, Tribology Letters, Computational Materials Science, and substantive talks at STLE or the Gordon Research Conference on Tribology.

## Skip

- Promoted or sponsored posts, LinkedIn News, ads.
- General AI business news (funding, stock prices, executive moves).
- General AI methods and applications unrelated to atomistic science or scientific AI computing infrastructure, such as chatbot releases, prompt engineering, tabular prediction benchmarks, text compression, robot perception, and world-model agents. Scientific studies of AI agents are not in this group; they are Adjacent.
- Scientific computing demonstrations outside the reader's research areas that do not report a relevant method or technical advance, such as running a tomography workflow across facilities.
- Broad battery and electrochemistry news without atomistic methods or relevant interface results, such as electric-vehicle launches and manufacturing announcements.
- General mechanical engineering or tribology posts without a substantive materials mechanism or research result, such as routine equipment maintenance and product marketing.
- Personal milestones and announcements: graduations, PhD defenses, new jobs, internships, awards, congratulations, memorials, job postings, networking invitations, and conference attendance without a substantive result. Save a post that itself reports a concrete relevant result, paper, dataset, tool, or technical talk.
- General outreach, conference announcements, art events, and casual discussions without a substantive relevant research result. Exception: posts from HPC and AI-for-science facilities (ALCF, Argonne, national labs, supercomputing centers) about new systems, capacity, workshops, or technical sessions are Adjacent.

## What counts as worth saving

Informative progress: a new paper or preprint, dataset, model release, software tool, benchmark, or substantive technical result or talk with enough content to learn from. A relevant paper can qualify even when its announcement gives only a concise description of the methods and research question.
